Question 1

What will my child learn?

Asking questions

Children ask Sunny, the chat helper, about things they wonder about and get a short answer with an everyday example.

Try: "How does a bicycle move?"

Explaining clearly

A good picture needs who, what, where and what the light is like. The Prompt Lab practises this step by step.

Try: a puppy, what it is doing, where.

Creating

Children turn their own ideas, or their own drawings, into finished pictures and short videos.

Try: a dragon who grows vegetables.

Checking answers

Sunny reminds children it is an AI and can be wrong. We suggest asking a grown-up, a teacher or a book about anything that matters.

Try: find something odd in a picture.

We have not measured learning results, and SparkGarden does not replace teachers or books. It is a way to practise talking about ideas.

These are all the activities in the app today.

Chat with Sunny

Ask questions, hear a story, get a joke or a riddle. Sunny uses short, simple words. A child can also share a picture and ask about it.

Example: "Why is the sky blue? Explain it like I am six."

Pictures

Describe a picture and the app makes it. A child can also upload their own drawing (smaller than 2 MB) and have it turned into a finished illustration.

Example: "A tiny dragon baking cookies, in a cosy kitchen at night."

Videos

Write or ask for a short script, then turn each scene into a video clip. {{CLIP_TEXT}}

Prompt Lab

A practice page. The child fills in cards (the idea, what is happening, who, where, the light) and watches a checklist and a written-out prompt build up. It also checks a short story for a good start, a hero, a problem, a surprise and an ending. It runs in the browser and costs nothing. One optional button, "Ask Sunny to review", sends the story to the AI and uses a little of the child's credits.

Examples and My Library

Examples are finished videos we chose, shown with the words and pictures that made them. They are read-only. My Library holds only what that child has made.

There is no chat between users, no sharing and no comments. A child cannot see anyone else's work.

AI Explorers: how does AI think?

A play-based area where kids learn how AI learns, decides, makes mistakes and gets biased.

Through short mini-games with a friendly robot buddy called Bolt, children discover that AI learns from examples, can get things wrong, and can be unfair when its examples are lopsided. The three mini-games work on the child's device only: nothing a child does in them is sent to our server, and progress stays on the device.

Two studios are different. Study Buddy (homework help and wonder questions) and Game Studio (a child describes a game and the AI builds it) send what the child types, and any picture they choose in Study Buddy, to an outside AI service so it can answer. They go through the same safety checks as the rest of the app. We do not ask for names, and the words are not saved on our server. If a message is blocked, we keep only a note of the kind of problem, not the words. A grown-up is asked to say OK before a child first opens either studio. Sunny is a computer program and can be wrong, and the studios gently remind children to check answers and to take a break.

An optional "Go further" link points to free Code.org courses. It is shown only after a grown-up taps a confirmation button.

Question 3

How do you prevent misuse?

Everything a child types, and everything the AI writes back, goes through checks. Here is what exists.

  1. Checks before anything is made. Each request passes two checks. The first is a word filter that sees through tricks such as swapped letters, spaces and look-alike characters. The second is an AI safety check that catches harder cases: hints, other languages and role-play. Both run before any picture, video or chat reply is made, and before any credits are used.
  2. What is blocked. Sexual content; anything sexual involving children; graphic violence; self-harm; illegal drugs; hate and harassment; bad language; and attempts to trick the checker. If the safety check cannot run, the request is blocked rather than allowed.
  3. Uploaded drawings and pictures go through an AI picture check. They are then re-saved as a plain, smaller picture, which removes location and camera details.
  4. The AI's own answers are checked too. If a chat answer or a written script is not safe, the child sees a short, safe message instead.
  5. Sunny's built-in rules. Sunny uses simple, kind words and avoids adult or frightening content. It does not ask for personal details, and it reminds children to keep their name, address, school and passwords private. If a child seems sad or unsafe, it encourages them to talk to a parent, teacher or other trusted adult. It does not follow instructions to ignore these rules.

What happens when a request is blocked

  • The child sees one friendly message and is not told which rule applied.
  • The request is not carried out and no credits are used.
  • The adult who runs the app can see it in a review list: what kind of request it was, where it happened and the first 80 characters. For Study Buddy and Game Studio the words are not kept, only the kind of problem.
  • After {{STRIKES}} blocked requests within 24 hours, the child's account is paused and signed out. An adult has to turn it back on.
  • Anything sexual involving children pauses the account on the first block.
  • Messages about hurting themselves are handled differently. The child is not penalised. They get a gentle message to talk to a trusted adult, and the message is recorded for the adult to see.

Outside services that receive some of it

To work, the app sends what the child types, and pictures they attach, to AI services run by other companies. Each company has its own privacy rules, which we do not control. We do not send your child's username or password.

  • Chat: the message goes to an outside AI service that writes the reply.
  • Pictures: the words, and any drawing the child attached, go to an outside AI service that makes the picture.
  • Videos: the scene words and any starting picture go to an outside AI service that makes the video.
  • Safety checks and story writing: the text being checked or written goes to an outside AI service.
  • Voice typing: only if the child taps the microphone. The audio goes to the browser's own speech service. In Chrome that is Google's.
  • Page code: each time the app opens, the browser loads standard open-source code libraries from a public hosting service.

There are no advertisements and no analytics or tracking tools in the app. It uses only the cookies needed to keep a child signed in.

Deleting

  • A child can delete their own pictures and videos from My Library, and delete chats from the sidebar.
  • You can ask the admin to pause the account at any time.
  • Removing an account and all its files completely is done by the person who runs the server, on request. There is no delete button yet.
Question 6

What can still go wrong?

AI can make mistakes. Safety checks can miss things. No system can promise that every answer is correct or that nothing unsuitable will ever get through.

  • An answer may contain a wrong fact. A picture may show something odd or wrong.
  • The checks look at the words and drawings that go in, and at written answers. They do not inspect the pictures and videos the AI makes. A picture can still come out odd or unsuitable.
  • A request that is fine may be blocked by mistake.
  • A child can still type personal details. Sunny will remind them not to, but cannot stop them.
  • The chat and picture AIs are run by other companies, and we rely on their services working properly.

Where a grown-up is still needed

  • Sit with your child for the first few sessions, and agree what they may make.
  • Talk about what they made: what worked, what looks wrong, what they would change.
  • Check anything important with a book, a teacher or a reliable site.
  • Look at their Library from time to time.
  • If something upsets them, stop, and tell the adult who runs SparkGarden so the account can be paused.

Questions parents ask

Who is SparkGarden for?

Sunny is written for children who want to learn how AI works, with a grown-up setting it up and staying nearby.

What information is saved?

Account details, the pictures and videos a child makes, and a short note of any blocked request. Chat conversations stay in the child's own browser. The full list is under Question 5.

How can I report a concern?

Tell the adult who gave your child their account. They can pause it straight away.

Does my child need supervision?

Yes, especially at first. Sit with them for the first few sessions, and keep checking in. The checks reduce the risk but do not remove it.

Does it cost money?

Nothing can be bought inside the app. Making things uses credits that an adult adds to the account.

Can my child talk to strangers?

No. There is no messaging, sharing or comments between users. Chat is only with Sunny, an AI.
